# Research summary, 2026-11-24

Short notes on how poker bots are evaluated and built, what fits a tight time budget, and what to try next.

## Evaluation methods

- **Duplicate dealing.** Same cards, seats swapped. The standard way to cut card luck. Used for every bench row.
- **AIVAT.** Variance reduction of more than 10x on match results: https://arxiv.org/abs/1612.06915
- **LBR (local best response).** An exploitability estimate: https://arxiv.org/pdf/1612.07547
- **SPRT.** A sequential test that stops a match when the result is clear: https://dannyhammer.github.io/engine-testing-guide/sprt.html
- **Slumbot API.** A public bot to benchmark against. Its status was not verified.
- **Sample size.** One Substack post cites about 100,000 hands before trusting a win rate. The bench uses 2,000 to 10,000 hands, so only large gaps are visible.

## How strong bots are built

- **Big systems** (Libratus, Pluribus, ReBeL) need seconds per decision and many cores.
- **Realistic under a tight clock:** precomputed tables plus a fast evaluator. Authors of an MIT competition entry report that a blueprint beats live heuristics under tight limits, and that plain equity misses overrealization.
- **Evaluators:** HenryRLee/PokerHandEvaluator (Apache-2.0), zekyll/OMPEval (ISC).
- **Avoid:** AGPL solvers (TexasSolver, postflop-solver).

## Short stacks and push/fold

- **Nash push/fold** for heads-up is a solved problem (Miltersen and Sorensen): https://www.itu.dk/~trbj/papers/poker.pdf
- No maintained open push/fold solver and no authoritative 12 to 20 bb chart were found.
- **Heads-up ICM** equals chip EV, so no ICM adjustment is needed.
- **Our own solved table lost to the heuristics on this bench** (see bench results, section c). A hybrid was mixed (section d). The lesson: shove against players who fold, open small against players who call.

## Robustness and opponent modelling

- **RNR and DBR** (robust and data-biased responses) need a solver.
- **Bayesian small-sample models** fit short matches best.
- **Exploit layer with a cap:** keep a safe base strategy and let the exploit move each threshold only so far.
- **Minimum sample:** do not switch on an opponent label until enough hands are seen.
- Cardot (2024): call-only and shove-only baselines, and switching between defensive and aggressive play: https://charlescardot.github.io/2024-05-04-Poker-Bot
- Rubin (MIT Pokerbots): Monte Carlo accuracy is not the bottleneck: https://www.mit.edu/~jlrubin/projects/pokerbots

## GitHub projects worth knowing

Metadata as of 2026-11-24. Several repos have under 10 stars, so stars say little about quality. READMEs were read; most code was not checked.

- https://github.com/gtowizard-ai/mitpoker-2024 (C++, MIT): blueprint-based competition bot.
- https://github.com/ericgjackson/slumbot2019 (C++, MIT).
- https://github.com/google-deepmind/open_spiel (Apache-2.0).
- https://github.com/mitpokerbots/pbots_calc (GPL-3.0, archived): ranged equity calculator. Read, do not copy.
- https://github.com/rselwyn/hunl-bot (no license): plain-words push/fold explanation.
- https://github.com/mbatv/fullhouse-engine (MIT): tournament engine for testing thresholds across many opponents.
- https://github.com/thotbreakerr/Texas-Holdem-AI (no license): a zoo of pluggable bots for regression testing.
- https://github.com/kailiu0712/LLM-based-Opponent-Modeling-in-Heads-up-Poker (no license): opponent archetypes (solver, nit, tag, lag, station) from running stats.
- https://github.com/rosbo/texas-holdem-poker-ai (MIT, stale): simulation plus opponent modelling in Java.
- https://github.com/girving/poker (stale): precomputed preflop equity table.
- Exploit-layer design: https://www.cs.auckland.ac.nz/research/gameai/publications/Norris_Watson_CIG13.pdf

**Ideas taken from them:** build an archetype test harness first; recency-weighted opponent stats with labels; a solved push/fold baseline; range-vs-range equity for shove and call decisions; an exploit layer with a safety cap.

## Substack ideas

- https://notpropoker.substack.com/p/youre-not-that-exploitable: stop bluffing stations and bet big for value; overbet bluffs against nits; raise size keyed to stack depth, with a min-raise at 15 bb or less.
- https://pokerfluency.substack.com/p/merging-gto-and-exploits: play near equilibrium on early streets, exploit more as the hand goes on, freely on the river.
- https://topuppoker.substack.com/p/poker-topic-for-2282024-poker-sample: sample sizes (paywalled, partial text).
- https://jamesin.substack.com/p/i-built-an-ai-system-to-analyze-poker: a per-hand log pipeline. Weak match, but the logging idea transfers.

**Ideas taken from them:** blend equity early with exploits on the river; classify the opponent as station, nit or always-raiser and switch bluff and value rules; require a minimum sample before switching; log a reference action per decision and audit disagreements.

## Effort estimate

Focused days: implement, test and bench. Tools speed up writing code, not validation.

| Tier | Items (days) | Total |
|---|---|---|
| Tier 1, do first | Match mode and SPRT (1.5-3); version vs version and round-robin (1-1.5); solved push/fold table (3-5, done 2026-11-24, lost to v3, parked); ship hygiene: SDK pin, validator layout, time fallback (1-1.5, pin and layout done) | about 7.5-11 days |
| Tier 2, stronger decisions | Bayesian opponent model with a style-switching bench opponent (3-5); board-aware range (5-9); adaptive aggression (2-4); mixed strategies for near-equal choices (1.5-2); faster evaluator and preflop cache (2-3) | about 13.5-23 days |
| Tier 3, research-grade | Short-stack CFR solve, 12-20 bb preflop (10-14); depth-limited postflop search under 100 ms (18-28); AIVAT (6-10); LBR exploitability (7-11); Slumbot benchmark (1-2, needs the API confirmed up) | about 42-65 days |
| Overall | | about 63-99 days |

Not recommended: a full precomputed blueprint like Slumbot or Libratus (2 to 3 months or more, plus large compute).

## C++ vs Python

Use a C++ hot loop for equity search and Python for the wire protocol and tooling. The platform requires Python for the SDK, and the sandbox blocks subprocess and ctypes.
